## 2. Unit-Economics Lessons from BYJU’S Collapse

| Metric | BYJU’S FY21 / FY22 | Lesson |
|--------|--------------------|--------|
| Ad spend | ₹2,250 Cr → ₹4,144 Cr | CAC exceeded LTV; unsustainable.[^7] |
| Ad spend / revenue | ~69 % | Buying growth, not earning it.[^7] |
| Net loss FY22 | ₹8,245 Cr | Valuation ≠ viability.[^7] |
| Debt | $1.2 Bn Term B loan | Leverage amplified downturn.[^7] |

**PhysicsWallah contrast:** negligible CAC via YouTube, ₹3K–4K course pricing, FY25 revenue ₹2,886 Cr with positive EBITDA.[^7]

## 3. Dr. Math Unit-Economics Targets

| Parameter | Conservative Target | Rationale |
|-----------|---------------------|-----------|
| Price to tuition centre | ₹50–150 / student / month | Below local worksheet/test-prep bundles |
| Price to school | ₹10–50 / student / month | Add-on to existing ERP/LMS |
| Gross margin | >70 % | Software-heavy; paper is customer’s cost |
| Payback period | <6 months | Aligns with annual school/tuition fee cycle |
| Churn | <15 % annually | Stickiness via adaptive loop + OMR history |

---

## 4. Sales Motion Implications

- **Land via tuition centres** with a low-risk pilot: free for one month, then per-student.
- **Expand to schools** once a centre’s outcome data is demonstrable.
- **Avoid heavy ad spend**; use teacher/principal referrals and local case studies.
- **Teacher-in-the-loop** reduces resistance and improves retention.
