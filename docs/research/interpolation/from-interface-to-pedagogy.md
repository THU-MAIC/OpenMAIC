# Interpolation Research — From Interface Access to Redefined Pedagogy

**Research method:** Interpolation — connecting the seminar’s separate observations into a coherent pedagogical arc.  
**Status:** Proposed

---

## 1. The Chain of Implications

The seminar makes several distinct points. Interpolating between them yields a single strategic chain:

```
Natural-language interface
        ↓
Democratised access to AI tools
        ↓
Automation of low-cost-of-error tasks
        ↓
Reclaimed teacher time and attention
        ↓
Shift from content delivery to higher-order skills
        ↓
Curriculum redesign around curiosity, judgment, empathy, creativity
```

Each link depends on the previous one. Interface access alone does not improve pedagogy; it only creates the **precondition** for redesign.

---

## 2. What Should Teachers Do With Reclaimed Time?

The seminar suggests the real purpose of education has always been deeper than the visible content:

| Surface activity | Underlying capability |
|------------------|-----------------------|
| Memorising state capitals | Memory discipline |
| Geometry proofs | Logical reasoning |
| Foreign language grammar | Cross-cultural communication |
| Calculus exercises | Problem-solving persistence |

If AI handles the surface activity, the teacher’s job becomes **making the underlying capability explicit and assessable** [S1].

---

## 3. A New Pedagogical Stack

Interpolating the seminar’s examples produces a three-layer stack:

### Layer 1 — AI handles the mundane
- Routine Q&A.
- Draft content and worksheets.
- First-pass feedback.
- Administrative emails.

### Layer 2 — Teacher designs the learning experience
- Higher-order problems.
- Peer instruction.
- Socratic dialogue.
- Project-based assessment.

### Layer 3 — Institution cultivates human-centric outcomes
- Curiosity and intrinsic motivation.
- Empathy and social-emotional skills.
- Ethical judgment.
- Cross-cultural competence.

This stack maps neatly onto the cost-of-error matrix: Layer 1 is mostly Quadrant 1/2, Layer 2 is Quadrant 3 with human-in-the-loop, and Layer 3 is largely Quadrant 4 where AI is only a support tool.

## 4. The “Calculator Analogy” Applied to Worksheets

The seminar uses calculators to argue that tools do not make subjects obsolete; they force subjects to evolve [S1].

For mathematics worksheets, the analogy implies:

| Old worksheet purpose | New worksheet purpose |
|-----------------------|-----------------------|
| Drill arithmetic | Build number sense and estimation |
| Test formula recall | Test problem formulation |
| Homework completion | Metacognitive reflection |
| Standardised practice | Personalised, adaptive challenge |

Dr. Math’s product design should therefore:

- Use AI to generate infinite practice at the right difficulty.
- Use OMR scanning to capture real performance data.
- Use adaptive algorithms to maintain productive struggle.
- Reserve teacher time for diagnosing **why** a student is stuck, not **what** question to ask next.

---

## 5. Implications for Curriculum Design

The seminar’s advice to parents—follow inspiring teachers, develop creativity, judgment, empathy, psychology—translates into curriculum design principles [S1]:

1. **Motivation-first:** Choose topics and contexts that spark curiosity.
2. **Transfer-focused:** Assess in novel contexts, not just repeated drills.
3. **Human-skill explicit:** Name and grade collaboration, explanation, and persistence.
4. **Ethics-integrated:** Teach students to evaluate AI outputs, not just consume them.
