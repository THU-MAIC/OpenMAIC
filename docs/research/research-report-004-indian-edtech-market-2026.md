## 1. Executive Summary

India’s EdTech sector has moved from the pandemic-era “Wild West” to a **post-BYJU’S correction** defined by profitable unit economics, phygital delivery, and institutional (B2B) sales. The direct-to-consumer online K-12 market is estimated at **$2.8–3.6 Bn in 2025**, with forecasts ranging from **$5–7 Bn by 2030** (RAYSolute) to **$33 Bn by 2034** (IMARC).[^7][^8]

**Key finding for Dr. Math:** The highest-leverage opportunity is **B2B2C**: selling an AI worksheet + OMR-scan + adaptive-remediation layer to tuition centres and budget private schools, rather than competing head-to-head with consumer apps like PhysicsWallah. Tuition-centre management software already costs ₹3,000–20,000/month; Dr. Math can position as a value-added module or independent AI grading layer.

**Recommended next step:** Define a pricing and packaging experiment for tuition chains (per-student-per-month, with a free tier for Natalmanjaya High School as a case study).

## 4. Product Implications

- **Avoid B2C CAC war:** PhysicsWallah owns low-CAC YouTube funnel; BYJU’S collapse showed the cost of buying growth.
- **Target institutional buyers:** Schools and tuition centres make centralized decisions and value outcome dashboards.
- **Price for India:** Mass-market worksheets must be priced below ₹500/student/month to compete with local tuition costs.
- **Build trust first:** Parent trust is the scarce resource post-BYJU’S; offer transparent grading, local language support, and teacher-in-the-loop controls.
- **Vernacular + voice:** Tier 2/3 adoption depends on Hindi/regional-language interfaces and low-bandwidth design.
