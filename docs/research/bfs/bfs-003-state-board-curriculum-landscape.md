**Date:** 2026-06-04  
**Research Phase:** BFS — breadth-first landscape mapping  
**Scope:** CBSE, Maharashtra (MSBSHSE), Karnataka (KSEAB), Tamil Nadu (DGE TN), Uttar Pradesh (UPMSP), Bihar (BSEB)

## 1. Board Snapshots

| Board | Class 10 Exam | Theory / Internal | Pass Marks | Notable 2025–26 Trait |
|-------|---------------|-------------------|------------|-----------------------|
| **CBSE** | AISSE | 80 / 20 | 33 % | Two board-exam sittings from 2025–26; Standard vs Basic math.[^1] |
| **MSBSHSE (Maharashtra)** | SSC | 80 / 20 | 35 % overall (28/80 theory) | Split into Algebra (40) + Geometry (40); includes GP and Cramer’s rule.[^2] |
| **KSEAB (Karnataka)** | SSLC | 80 / 20 | 35 % | No syllabus change; NCERT-based but board-specific marks distribution.[^3] |
| **DGE TN (Tamil Nadu)** | SSLC | 100 / 0 (Maths) | 35 % | Samacheer Kalvi textbooks; no internal assessment for Maths.[^4] |
| **UPMSP (Uttar Pradesh)** | High School | 70 / 30 | 33 % | Follows NCERT pattern; 30-mark project/internal component.[^5] |
| **BSEB (Bihar)** | Matric | Theory-heavy | 33 % | Bihar SCERT textbooks; more direct/textbook-based than CBSE.[^6] |

## 3. Exam-Pattern Observations

- **CBSE** emphasizes competency-based questions; ~54 % understanding, ~24 % application, ~22 % higher-order skills.[^1]
- **Maharashtra** has a rigid Algebra/Geometry split (40 marks each) and asks 25–35 questions including MCQs, short and long answers.[^2]
- **Karnataka** and **Tamil Nadu** papers are 100 marks; Tamil Nadu Maths has no internal assessment, while Karnataka gives 20 marks internal.[^3][^4]
- **UPMSP** retains a 70/30 split with substantial project/internal weight, making worksheet practice only one input.[^5]
- **Bihar** is perceived as more textbook-oriented and less application-heavy than CBSE.[^6]

## 5. Strategic Takeaways

1. **CBSE is the de facto competitive baseline** — JEE/NEET foundation is NCERT-aligned, so CBSE sequencing is a safe default.
2. **State boards are closer to CBSE than to each other in content**, but diverge in chapter ordering and weightage.
3. **Language medium matters** — state boards offer Hindi, Marathi, Kannada, Tamil, Urdu etc.; question wording and distractors should be generated in the student’s medium.
4. **Internal/project assessment is significant** in UP, Maharashtra, Karnataka; Dr. Math worksheets should optionally include “practice for internal assessment” tagging.
